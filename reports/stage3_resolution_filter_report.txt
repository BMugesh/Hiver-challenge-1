4. Resolution Policy: Why 'Support Replied' != 'Resolved'
------------------------------
In customer support datasets, a support agent reply does NOT automatically indicate resolution:
 1. Channel Redirects: 62.15% of threads end with AppleSupport directing the user to DM
    (e.g., '@customer Please DM us your serial number: https://t.co/...'). The public thread ends
    at the handoff point; the actual technical resolution happens out-of-channel.
 2. Diagnostic Abandonment: 5.00% of threads end after AppleSupport asks a clarifying question
    (e.g., 'Which iOS version are you running?') and the user never responds.
 3. High-Quality Evidence Standard: A conversation is only qualified as reliable historical
    resolution evidence when AppleSupport provided concrete technical instructions/links,
    and/or the customer explicitly confirmed the fix.

5. Resolution Categories Defined
------------------------------
 - CLEARLY_RESOLVED   : Explicit customer confirmation of fix, gratitude for working solution, or verified resolution.
 - PARTIALLY_RESOLVED : Concrete self-contained troubleshooting steps/links provided without explicit customer confirmation.
 - ESCALATED          : AppleSupport requested user to move to Direct Message (DM) or escalated out-of-channel.
 - ABANDONED          : Customer stopped responding after AppleSupport asked a diagnostic question.
 - UNCLEAR            : Inconclusive, ambiguous, or open-ended dialogue without confirmed resolution.

12. Known Limitations & Selection Bias Disclosure
------------------------------
 Resolved historical evidence is a deliberate design choice and introduces selection bias:
  1. Resolved conversations are naturally longer (mean 4.30 turns vs 2.76 turns for excluded)
     because observing resolution requires problem definition -> instruction -> verification.
  2. In-channel Twitter resolutions skew toward configuration/how-to and software glitches,
     whereas hardware replacements and billing disputes are almost always escalated to DM.
